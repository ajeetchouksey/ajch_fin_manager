What you're saving for

Each goal becomes one number: how much to put aside per month. Inflation and growth are included.

Which growth rate should I use?

  • Within 3 years — keep it safe: Tagesgeld 2–3%, Indian FD 6–7%.
  • 5+ years — a global equity ETF has averaged ~6–8% a year over long periods, but can drop 30–50% in a bad year.
  • Inflation — ~2% for euro goals, ~5% for rupee goals.

Goal ideas for your situation

  • Emergency fund — 6 months of spending in Tagesgeld.
  • Your child's education — 12–15 years away → equity ETF Sparplan.
  • Retirement top-up — the gap between your expected German pension and the income you want.
  • Flat interiors & possession costs — furniture, maintenance deposit, registration.

    Rules of thumb

    Spread the risk

    One all-world ETF owns ~4,000 companies. A single stock can go to zero.

    Fees compound too

    A 1.5% yearly fee eats about a quarter of your gains over 20 years. Look for TER under 0.3%.

    Tax in Germany

    ~26.4% on gains when you sell; the first €2,000 a year (joint) is tax-free with Freistellungsaufträge.

    Indian funds from Germany

    Non-EU funds get complex German fund taxation — check with an advisor before adding more.

    Rupee vs euro

    INR has lost roughly 3–4% a year against EUR. Rupee returns look bigger than they are in euros.

    Time, not timing

    Monthly investing beats waiting for the “right moment” for most people.

    🇩🇪 Tax on investment income

    Cross-border checklist

    • NRE interest: tax-free in India, taxable in Germany → Anlage KAP.
    • NRO / FD interest: bank deducts ~31%; treaty rate is 10% → give the bank Form 10F + German tax-residence certificate, or claim it back in your ITR.
    • Resident accounts must become NRO once you're an NRI (FEMA).
    • Sending money India → Germany from NRO needs Form 15CA/15CB.
    • Keep every Gera receipt and TDS challan — you need them to work out gains if you ever sell.
